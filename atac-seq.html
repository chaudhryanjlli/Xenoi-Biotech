About this workshop

ATAC-Seq maps open chromatin, the parts of the genome that are physically accessible to regulatory proteins, using a small amount of cells and a short protocol. Accessible regions often mark active promoters and enhancers, so the data reveals which parts of the genome a cell type is using. This workshop covers the analysis from raw reads to interpretation: quality checks that use fragment-size patterns, alignment and filtering, peak calling and building a reliable set of accessible regions. You then annotate peaks, search for enriched transcription factor motifs, examine footprints that hint at bound factors, and test for regions that open or close between conditions. Finally you learn to link accessibility to nearby genes and compare it with expression.

Tools and pipelines you will use

This workshop emphasizes standard, open-source bioinformatics tools widely used in peer-reviewed genomics literature:

FastQC ↗Bowtie2 ↗Picard ↗MACS3 ↗deepTools ↗HOMER ↗TOBIAS ↗DiffBind ↗ChIPseeker ↗

Frequently asked questions

Is this ATAC-Seq workshop delivered online?

Yes. All Xenoi Biotech workshops are delivered 100% online, allowing researchers and students across India and internationally to participate with remote compute support.

How long is the workshop and what is the workload?

The workshop runs for 30 days across 6 structured modules. Learners typically dedicate 1 to 2 flexible hours per day to work through pipeline commands, code exercises, and the mini-project.

Who is this workshop designed for?

It is built for B.Sc., M.Sc., and Ph.D. students, postdoctoral researchers, faculty members, and industry professionals in biotechnology, biochemistry, microbiology, and medicine who need to analyze NGS datasets independently.

What will I learn in this workshop?

You will master the full open chromatin analysis pipeline from raw FASTQ quality control, Bowtie2 alignment, and mitochondrial read filtering to MACS3 peak calling, transcription factor motif discovery, digital footprinting with TOBIAS, and differential accessibility testing using DiffBind.
