About this workshop

Targeted metagenomics identifies the microbes in a sample by sequencing one marker gene, the 16S rRNA gene for bacteria and archaea or the ITS region for fungi. It is a common first step in microbiome studies of gut, soil, water and plant samples. This workshop shows how to turn amplicon reads into results: removing primers, denoising reads into exact sequence variants, removing chimeras and assigning taxonomy against a reference database. You then describe diversity within and between samples, make ordination plots and test which taxa differ between groups, with attention to the pitfalls of compositional data and unequal sequencing depth. You also learn to predict likely community functions from the marker data and to present the results clearly.

Our research: Explore our team's published microbiome investigations: Tina Roy et al. What you will learn

Module 1 Foundations
Week 1
Linux and command-line basics Run tools and manage files.
Microbiome concepts Marker genes and what amplicon data can tell you.

Read quality control Inspect and clean reads.
Module 2 Amplicon processing
Primer removal and trimming Clean amplicon reads.
Denoising Infer exact sequence variants.
Merging and chimera removal Build clean feature tables.
Feature table Count each variant per sample.
Module 3 Taxonomy

Taxonomic assignment Label variants with likely organisms.
Filtering Remove contaminants and rare noise.

Module 4 Diversity

Beta diversity Compare communities between samples.
Ordination plots Visualize community differences.
Rarefaction and normalization Handle unequal sequencing depth.

Comparing groups Test which taxa differ.

Predicted function Infer pathways from marker genes.
Visualization Bar plots, heatmaps and cladograms.

Frequently asked questions

Is this Targeted Metagenomics (16S / ITS) workshop delivered online?

Yes. All Xenoibiotech workshops are delivered 100% online, allowing researchers and students across India and internationally to participate with remote compute support.

How long is the workshop and what is the workload?

The workshop runs for 30 days across 6 structured modules. Learners typically dedicate 1 to 2 flexible hours per day to work through pipeline commands, code exercises, and the mini-project.

Who is this workshop designed for?

It is built for B.Sc., M.Sc., and Ph.D. students, postdoctoral researchers, faculty members, and industry professionals in biotechnology, biochemistry, microbiology, and medicine who need to analyze NGS datasets independently.

What will I learn in this workshop?

You will learn demultiplexing, primer trimming, DADA2 amplicon sequence variant (ASV) inference, taxonomic classification against SILVA/Greengenes, alpha and beta diversity metrics, PCoA ordination plots, and functional metagenome prediction with PICRUSt2.
